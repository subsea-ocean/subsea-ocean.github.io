<p style="

  line-height:1.8;
">

I am a marine biologist from Argentina, currently a PhD candidate supported by CONICET, where I study coastal planktonic communities and how they respond to environmental variability.

</p>

<p style="
  font-size:1.05em;
  line-height:1.8;
">

I grew up far from the ocean, in a landlocked region of the country, but early experiences with the sea sparked a deep curiosity that eventually shaped my scientific path.

</p>

<p style="
  font-size:1.05em;
  line-height:1.8;
">

My research focuses on plankton — the tiny organisms that form the base of marine food webs and play a key role in ocean health and climate regulation. By studying plankton in dynamic coastal environments, such as surf zones and shallow waters, I aim to understand how physical processes, storms, and human pressures influence marine ecosystems at their most fundamental level.

</p>

<p style="
  font-size:1.05em;
  line-height:1.8;
">

I am particularly interested in connecting detailed scientific research with broader environmental questions, including biodiversity conservation and the impacts of global change on coastal systems.

</p>

<p style="
  font-size:1.05em;
  line-height:1.8;
">

Beyond research, I strongly value science communication and outreach. I believe that sharing how science is done, why it matters, and who does it is essential for building stronger links between science and society.

</p>

<p style="
  font-size:1.05em;
  line-height:1.8;
">

Participating in the R/V <i>Falkor</i> (too) expedition represents an extraordinary opportunity to expand my training, collaborate with an international team, and bring back not only data and experience, but also stories that can inspire others — especially students from regions where the ocean once felt distant to them, just as it did for me.

</p>
